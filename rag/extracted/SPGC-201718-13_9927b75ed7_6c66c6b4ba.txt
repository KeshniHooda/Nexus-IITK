Item 4 Items Requiring SPGC Recommendations for Consideration of Chairperson Senate / Senate

The SPGC considered the various items and makes recommendations as noted

(a) To consider the recommendations of the sub-committee on apparent anomalies/ambiguities in the PG Manual.
The subcommittee recommendations have been duly deliberated by the SPGC. Details of the proposal can be seen
in Annexure-1.

5   Policy matters requiring detailed discussions.
(a) To consider the proposal for provision of Paternity Leave and enhancement of duration of Maternity Leave from
90 days to 180 days.
The SPGC recommends adoption of a new paternity leave clause and a revised maternity leave clause for PG
students. These recommendations are in line with extant revised norms for Government of India staff, including
our faculty and staff. Details of the proposal can be seen in Annexure-2.
(b) To discuss on enabling Clause ‘Direct Post-Graduate Admission Offers after competitive examination results’.
The SPGC, after due deliberation, is recommending an enabling clause that would allow departments/IDPs to
make admission offers to their PG programs immediately post declaration of results for reputed PG admission
competitive examination results such as GATE, NET etc. The primary motivation is to improve the quality of
student intake via early offers. Details of the proposal can be seen in Annexure-3

(c) To consider the proposal for ‘Thesis Pre-Processing Towards Maintaining Academic Standards’.

The SPGC has deliberated at length on maintaining thesis standards, particularly in light of concerns on originality
/ plagiarism, non-technical aspects such as a professional get-up (formatting etc) as well as proper thesis revision
in response to external review. The relevant SPGC proposal can be seen in Annexure-4. The proposal  is
recommended for consideration of the Senate. Portions that do not require the Senate approval should be
implemented.

(d) To discuss ‘Thesis external evaluation procedure revise’.

The SPGC has reviewed the time taken in completing the external review of PhD theses for degrees conferred in
the Convocation this year. With more than 35% of the theses requiring above four months for external review
completion, the SPGC is of the view that such a long lag-time is not desirable in today’s times. The SPGC further

“A male student may be granted paternity leave for a maximum of 15 working days each for up to two children. Such

combined with any other leave due and will not entail any loss of financial assistantship”.

Annexure - 3

DIRECT PG ADMISSION OFFERS POST COMPETITIVE EXAMINATION RESULTS

With the intention of improving the quality of intake of Master's/PhD students, the Senate is requested to approve an
enabling provision whereby, immediately post declaration of a competitive examination result (e.g. GATE, NET etc), a
department/IDP can make admission offers into its PG programs to the toppers of the competitive examination(s). The idea
is to catch the attention of the brightest students through an early offer. The tentative modus operandi is as follows:

  Admission committee(s) is (are) duly approved a few days before declaration of competitive examination results.
   Official merit list is obtained (e.g. from GATE Chairman) within a day of declaration of results.
  Committee recommends admission strictly in ascending order of rank in all categories.
  Number of offers is limited by the number of seats in each category. Attrition may be permitted on request. Total number
of offers made must follow applicable reservation rules.
  PG10 is approved by Chairman, Senate
  Admission offer letters are sent to admitted candidates within a week of exam result declaration.
  To accept the admission, offered students are required to deposit (prorated) fee by ~April 15th (this is by the deadline
for online application closure into our Master's programs). Any minor discrepancy in the actual fee payable is to be
adjusted at the time of registration.
  The offer is considered as withdrawn to students who do not pay the fee by the ~April 15 deadline.
   Regular admissions to applications received against our usual advertisement are offered against vacant seats with
applicable attrition

Please note that the above is only an enabling provision and departments/IDPs shall be free to invoke it at their sole discretion.

Proposed Enabling Provision:

"Immediately post declaration of results of reputed competitive examinations for admission into post-graduate programs (eg

GATE, NET etc), interested departments/IDPs are permitted to admit examination toppers to their post-graduate programs,

strictly in the order of ascending ranks in the examination. The number of such offers shall be limited by the number of

available seats in each category with applicable attrition (if any). Total number of offers made must follow applicable

reservation rules. The competitive examination should preferably be an eligibility requirement for admission into at least

one of the PG programs of the department/IDP".

Annexure-4

PROPOSAL FOR ADDRESSING SPECIFIC CONCERNS ON SUBMITTED THESES

Keeping in view the ideal of maintaining the highest standards and integrity of research output from IITK, the following
measures are proposed by the SPGC

1. Originality and Plagiarism Concerns
(a) The thesis should contain a signed “Declaration” by the student. The “Declaration” is provided in Annexure A.
(b) The student must submit a signed “Certificate” to be kept in the student file. The “Certificate” is provided in Annexure
B
(c) Soft copy of the thesis must be subjected to a similarity check by an Institute recommended “similarity checker”. If the
similarity index is above a minimum threshold, the thesis shall not be accepted for further processing. Currently, the
Institute uses I-thenticate and Turnitin as similarity checkers.

2. Professional Thesis Getup
(a) The thesis must follow the Institute recommended formatting guidelines, with particular adherence to consistency
across the entire thesis. Items requiring particular attention are noted in Annexure C.
(b) The thesis is readable with reasonable English usage (eg acceptable "grammarly" and "spell check" reports).

3. Revising thesis in light of external referee reports
(a) In addition to a “response to referee comments”, a soft copy of the revised thesis clearly highlighting the revisions
incorporated (eg in different font color such as blue) must be provided. This highlighted revised thesis soft copy shall
be made available to the oral board examiners as well as external referee for a Category III report, for their reference.
This is in addition to the current practice for providing the “response to referee comments”.

To ensure effective implementation of items 1(c), 2 and 3 above, a “thesis processing cell” may be constituted in PG office
with stipulated maximum time limits on the different processing steps.

Annexure – 5

REDUCING THE THESIS EXTERNAL REVIEW PROCESS COMPLETION TIME

Background

The reviewed data clearly shows that:
  More than 35% theses take above 4 months for external review completion.
  The average response time of foreign referees is 20+ days less than the Indian referee.
   If only two reviews were required, the average external review completion time will be more than a month
lower (79 days for 2 reviews vs 116 days for 3 reviews)

SPGC View

The SPGC is of the firm view that when journals have moved from 3-6 months manuscript review period~15
years ago, to 3-6 weeks today, it is not healthy for IITK to have a thesis external review average lag time of ~4
months.

Recommendation

With a view to significantly reducing the lag time, the SPGC suggests that:

  An automated online thesis review portal be developed on priority with automated reminders to the reviewers.
In the interim, a stop-gap automated arrangement should be put in place.
  Revise the external thesis review process so that in case the first two received reports are positive (not
category III), the referee whose report is awaited is given 2 weeks to submit the review (assuming s/he is past
the deadline). If the referee still does not respond, the thesis processing need not be further delayed and the
thesis defence may proceed armed with the two positive reviews.

The detailed revised step-by-step thesis external review process is as below:
1. The three referees are approached, as we do now.
2. At the time of approaching the referees, they are gently intimated that they may be requested to serve on the
PhD thesis oral board with due remuneration and that by consenting to review the thesis, they are also
expressing their willingness to serve on the oral board. Referees from abroad will also be intimated that their
participation in the oral board examination, should they be requested, shall be over video conferencing only.
3.  In case the first two referee reports are positive (i.e. not category III) and both reports are from abroad, then
the higher priority referee (as per Chairman, Senate’s original prioritization) is requested to join the thesis
oral examination board. In case one of the reports is from within India, the Indian examiner is requested to
serve on the oral board.
4.  Concurrently, the remaining third referee is given a final deadline to submit the review. This deadline shall
be two weeks from the receipt of the second report or the originally conveyed deadline, whichever is later.
The polite reminder shall clearly specify that the given deadline is final and in case we do not hear from
him/her, we shall assume that s/he is not in a position to review the thesis and proceed further with the
processing of the thesis.
5. One week before the deadline, a final reminder is sent to the referee whose review is awaited.
6.  In case referee does not submit review by the final deadline, a very polite message is sent to him/her saying
that while we appreciate the time and energy that may have been spent in reviewing the thesis, in the interest
of time, it shall not be possible for us to wait further for his/her review. We hope he would be willing to oblige
us with a timely review the next time.
7. Armed with two positive (non category III) referee reports and respective student responses, the process for
thesis oral defence is initiated by the thesis supervisor.
