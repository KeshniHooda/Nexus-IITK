8.  Course Description:

A) Objectives: Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:
•  Explain semiconductor packaging hierarchies, package architectures, materials, and
emerging technology trends.
•  Identify the  electrical, thermal, and mechanical  factors governing semiconductor
package performance.
•  Analyze  the  effects  of  material  properties,  thermal  expansion  mismatch,  and
thermomechanical stresses on package reliability.
•  Understand finite element-based modeling approaches for evaluating package stresses
and predicting solder joint fatigue life.
•  Apply  statistical methods, including Weibull analysis and lifetime  distributions, to
interpret reliability data and estimate component lifetime.
•  Evaluate  design  challenges and  reliability  considerations  in advanced  packaging
technologies, including 2.5D/3D integration, chiplets, and hybrid bonding.

C) Pre-requisites, if any: None

D) Short summary for including in the Courses of Study Booklet: This course provides an
overview of semiconductor packaging architectures, materials, and design principles,
with emphasis on  electrical, thermal, and mechanical performance.  It introduces
package-level   reliability  mechanisms,  finite  element-based   reliability  modeling,
statistical life data analysis, and emerging advanced packaging technologies. Through
engineering concepts and case studies, students will develop an understanding of the
relationship between package design, material properties, performance, and long-term
reliability.
