Subject - Discussion of the ARC report in the Students’ Senate.

Dear Sir,

This is to inform you that the ARC committee report was discussed in the Students’ Senate in its
4th Senate meeting held on 13th September 2009. The senate was appreciative of the eﬀort put by
the ARC committee and the suggestions that the committee came up with despite there being no
student representatives in the committee for most part of the committees tenure. The senate was
in approval of most of the suggestions in the ARC report. However, there were concerns raised on
the warning/AP system suggested in the ARC report and the compulsary attendance policy. Hence
a committee was formulated to look further into these matters and come up with suggestions. The
senate is not in the favour of a forced attendance policy and feels that alternative measures can be
taken to address the associated problems. Some of these measures have been suggested in the report
but we believe that much better solutions would emerge with discussion between the students and
the administration. It is hoped that the administration would provide for such avenues.  I request
you to consider the report attached.

I.  Attendance Policy

The student community feels that forcing a policy is not in the right spirit and instead the
student community will take steps on its own part to improve the attendance in the classes.
Below are enlisted just a few of the many ways / suggestions that will have a definite positive
impact on attendance.

•   Limiting Gymkhana Activities to 12:00 Midnight

All Students’ Gymkhana activities shall be strongly discouraged after 12 Midnight. This
however shall be applicable only when the next day is a working day. Special
guidelines shall be set up for all the clubs in order to ensure the same.

•  Academic Area Ambiance Proposal

One reason why students don’t attend classes is because of poor time tabling, with
free slots in between they usually go back to their hostel rooms and are too tired to
return for the next class.

By increasing the places where students can stay back in the Academic Area during
the Free Slots will certainly have a positive impact on the attendance of students,
besides raising their concentration and atmosphere in the classroom.

•  Encourage use of Laptops/Net books/ Notebooks in Classrooms

It is found that a lot of students prefer studying on their laptops, i.e. using laptops for e-
books or accessing other study material. By encouraging the use of Laptops/Netbooks
in Wi-Fied Classrooms, would increase the standard of teaching and the kind of doubts
asked in the class. Hence leaving no substitute to classroom education and thus
encouraging students to attend classes.

•  Class Timings

o   Winter Class Timings

It is observed that the attendance drops further in the Winter Semester (Even
Semester) this is primarily because of the extreme cold weather of Kanpur.
One solution to this would be to shift the classes by one hour i.e. instead of
being held from 0800-1700 Hrs, they be held from 0900-1800 Hrs. This would
without doubt have an impact on the attendance in classes.

o   Laboratory Timings

It is also suggested that Laboratories be shifted to the morning slot i.e. from
0800-1300 Hrs. This will indirectly force the students to get up early ! It is also
observed that a few departments have field work to be done as a part of
their laboratory; it would be much more comfortable for the students and the
Laboratory in charge if the laboratories are shifted to the morning slot.
Preferably from 1000-1300 Hrs.

o  New Class Timing Slot from 2000-2300 Hrs.

It was also felt that a new Time Slot be offered, from 2000-2300 Hrs. This is
without doubt is a time when a lot of students would like to attend classes.
However it was also felt that this may interfere with a few extracurricular

activities. And hence the courses that would be offered in this slot be limited
to Open Electives and HSS (Level 2) courses. This will also in some sense

o   5 Minute Break in 1.5 Hour Classes

It is observed that it becomes a little difficult to concentrate in an 1.5 hour
class, it is thus suggested that a five minute break be given in between class.

II.  Warning System

The committee felt that the system of Warning can be done away with to the extent that no
warning should be issued to the student.

Instead a more comprehensive Grade Sheet should be sent to all the students. The grade
sheet shall include the following features:

•  Grades in all the subjects of all the semesters.

•   SPI of all the semesters, and the CPI.

•  A more detailed description of each grade both in English and Hindi. (If possible this may
be extended to other languages also)

•  Graphs indicating Institute Average for that particular batch and Departmental Average
for that particular Batch.

•  A line indicating the performance of the student in the previous semester.

The Counselling Service could help the DOAA Office with the same. It is also
recommended that only one intimation be sent to parents from the DOAA Office,
instead of two which is in practice right now (one from the DOAA Office and the other
from Counselling Service).

The Line for the students, who fall into the current Warning Criteria, should be a well
chosen one, one which puts the entire situation in the right perspective. We suggest
the Line to be :

“The performance of your ward has been unsatisfactory in the last semester and we
request you to kindly look into the same and motivate your ward to work harder.”
