6. Course Description:

A) Objectives:

This course explores the rich and formative period of Western philosophy, tracing its origin
and evolution. It begins by considering how philosophy emerged from and contended with
myths and poetry, while also intertwining with natural sciences in its early stages. The course
investigates the profound questions posed by the Greeks that remain relevant today: Why is
there something rather than nothing? What is the origin of existence? Can we identify the
building blocks of reality? Can nature guide us in living a meaningful life? What constitutes a
good life? What is the ideal form of government? Is poetry inherently irrational? Students
will engage in a contemporary and critical dialogue with the Pre-Socratics, Sophists,
Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle. The course discusses the interplay between inquiries into the
natural world and the social-cultural realm, demonstrating how these domains influence one
another. The course shows how a philosophical conversation with ancient thinkers can be
intellectually enriching and transformative.
