6. Course Description: Elective for Ph.D., Masters, and senior UG students in mechanical engineering, and other
students interested in mechanics, applied mathematics, materials science, and related areas.

(A) Objectives:

Real elastic solids are rarely perfectly homogeneous. Their mass density, elastic moduli, microstructure, geometry,
defects, and interfaces exhibit spatial variability. When this variability is modeled probabilistically, the equations
of elasticity become stochastic, leading to a mechanics of random elastic media. Such models arise in composites,
porous and heterogeneous materials, polycrystals, metamaterials, granular media, damaged solids, and materials
with uncertain microstructure.

• To introduce the role of randomness and uncertainty in mechanical systems and to distinguish deterministic
mechanics from stochastic mechanics and random-media models.

• To introduce random mechanical structures, including random walks, graphs, lattices, point processes, random
fields, random tensors, random matrices, and random interfaces.

• To connect microscopic structural randomness and material heterogeneity with macroscopic mechanical response
through equilibrium, wave propagation, homogenization, instability, fracture, and reliability concepts.

• To introduce computational and data-driven tools for stochastic mechanics, including Monte Carlo simulation,
random-field representations, and physics-informed/data-driven modelling.

The proposed course develops mechanics starting from the equations of elasticity and uses random media as the
central theme.  This connects probability with elastic equilibrium, wave propagation, homogenization, effective
constitutive laws, scattering, localization, and computational mechanics.

(C) Prerequisites, if any: Instructor consent; familiarity with basic mechanics, multivariable calculus, differential
equations, linear algebra, and elementary probability is desirable.

(D) Short summary for inclusion in the Courses of Study Booklet:

This course develops the mechanics of elastic media with random microstructure, geometry, material properties,
or loading. It introduces random fields and correlations and studies their effects on elasticity, waves, and effective
properties through homogenization. Topics include random lattices, scattering, localization, fluctuations, defects,
and fracture, with computational tools.

7. Recommended Textbooks/References

(a) Torquato, S. (2002). Random Heterogeneous Materials: Microstructure and Macroscopic Properties, Springer.

(b) Beran, M. J. (1965). Statistical Continuum Theories, Interscience/Wiley.

(c) Mura, Toshio. Micromechanics of defects in solids. Springer Science & Business Media, 2013.

(d) Treloar, L. R. G. (1975). The Physics of Rubber Elasticity, 3rd Edition, Oxford University Press.

(e) Selected recent literature on random lattices, wave scattering, stochastic elasticity, Bayesian methods, and
data-driven methods will be provided by the instructor. Example: Effective dynamics in lattices with random
mass perturbations, arXiv:2309.03090.

8. Any other remarks:

The course is interdisciplinary. It is designed to serve as a common platform connecting solid mechanics, materials,
lattice dynamics, computational mechanics, and data-driven modelling. The content can be adapted for a more
mathematical or more engineering-oriented offering depending on the background of the enrolled students.
