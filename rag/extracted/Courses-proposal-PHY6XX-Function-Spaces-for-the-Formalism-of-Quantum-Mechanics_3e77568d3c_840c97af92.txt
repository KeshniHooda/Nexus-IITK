9. Course Outline

(a) Introduction [7 Lectures]

Basic axioms of linear vector spaces and the axioms of quantum mechanics (QM)
Basic properties of functions and establishing the functions as vectors in inﬁnite
dimensional linear vector space, connection to bras and kets in QM
Sequence of functions and convergence criterion of the sequence and the concept
of a metric
Theory of generalized functions, generally used in QM, with a special emphasize
on Dirac-delta distribution
The Dirac notation and the reasons why it works
Matrix elements and expectation values

(b) Basic theory of integration of functions [3 Lectures]

Normalization of wavefunctions in QM and Riemann integrable functions
Failure of Riemann integrability
Introduction to integration measure theory in general
Brief introduction to path integral measure in QM

(c) Brief introduction to Lebesgue measure and Lebesgue integration [4]

Introduction to Lebesgue integration theory and its application
Properties of Lebesgue integrals and their relationship with Riemann integrals

(d) Hilbert space [7 Lectures]

Deﬁnition of an inner product in a function space and its obvious connection
with QM
Introduction to Hilbert spaces, in particular L2[R]
Distinction between the Banach space L1[R] and L2[R]
The contents of Lp normed spaces in brief

Norm induced by the inner products and the concept of orthonormal basis used
in QM
The concept of the dual vector space and their relationship with ’bra’ vectors of
Dirac
The various basic inequalities as: Minkowski inequality, Cauchy-Schwarz in-
equality and Holder’s inequality

(e) Fourier series and Fourier transforms [3 Lectures]

Deﬁnition of a generalized Fourier series and Fourier transform and their con-
nection with the basics of QM
Discussion on the convergence properties of the Fourier series and Fourier trans-
forms and their connection with Bessel’s inequality and Parseval relation
Norm preserving Fourier transforms and L2 spaces and the relationship between
the position and momentum basis in quantum mechanics
Fourier transforms and wave packets in QM

(f) Linear operators in function space [7 Lectures]

Bounded operators in function spaces
Diﬃculties with unbounded operators in quantum mechanics
The position and momentum operators as unbounded operators
Self-adjoint bounded operators in QM
Diﬃculty with self-adjoint unbounded operators and Stone-von Neumann theo-
rem and application in basics of QM
The Hellinger-Toeplitz theorem on symmetric operators and its huge implication
in QM
Self-adjoint extension of symmetric operators in quantum mechanics

(g) Eigenvalue spectrum of Hermitian operators [8 Lectures]

The eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of linear operators in function spaces and in
particular their relevance in QM
The spectral theorem for bounded linear operators
Some remarks on the spectral theorem for unbounded operators and their im-
plications in QM and other branches of physics

10. Prerequisites: Mathematical Method I, Quantum Mechanics I

11. Short summary for the course of study booklet: While teaching quantum mechan-
ics or Mathematical methods one does not get enough time to emphasize on the particular
diﬃculties one faces when problems purely related to functional spaces arise. These prob-
lems are not at all esoteric in nature, the problems are there right in the ﬁrst postulates
of quantum mechanics where one assumes a fundamental commutation relation between
unbounded operators. The problems again come when one has to deﬁne self-adjointness
of unbounded operators in quantum mechanics. In quantum mechanics most of the sim-
ple operators as position, momentum or even the Hamiltonian turn out to be unbounded
operators. To have a proper understanding of these operators and the Hilbert space on
which they act, one must have to have some introductory understanding of functional
space. This course is aimed to infuse some of these ideas which are very important for a
physicist who wants to have a holistic grasp on quantum mechanics/quantum ﬁeld theory.
