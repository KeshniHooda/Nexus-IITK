Course Description :

As MOSFETs scale, their architecture evolves to overcome increasingly fundamental limits in
electrostatics, transport,  variability, and manufacturability. This course aims to develops the
device physics needed to understand the evolution from bulk MOSFETs to modern 3D transistors,
2D materials-based devices, and emerging More-than-Moore technologies.

The course will begin with the review of MOSFET scaling and short-channel eﬀects, and examines
the architectural innovations developed to address them. It will progress from bulk MOSFETs to
PDSOI and FDSOI, FinFETs, nanosheet and nanowire FETs, and complementary FETs (CFETs).
The course will develop the fundamentals of electrostatics and carrier transport in thin and highly
conﬁned channels, including the eﬀects of geometry, conﬁnement, band structure, mobility,
defects, surface roughness, and device variability. Approaches for modeling and simulating these
eﬀects will also be introduced.

The course will then explores the atomic limit of channel scaling through 2D materials.  It will
covers representative material systems such as graphene,  transition-metal dichalcogenides
(TMDs), and  hexagonal boron  nitride  (hBN),  focusing on  their  electronic  properties  i.e.
electrostatics, and transport. Particular attention  will be given to the challenges of realizing
practical 2D devices, including contacts, gate dielectrics, substrates, defects and doping.

The course concludes with an introduction to More-than-Moore device concepts, such as
realizing new material system with 2D materials based hetro-integrations, leveraging the defect in
these materials to build new devices for neuromorphic computing, leveraging phase change and
spin to realise new devices, and other emerging device concepts for unconventional computing
and specialized applications.

The main objective is to develop an understanding of device physics beyond the bulk MOSFET
and the technological directions shaping future transistors. The course also aims to provide
students with a framework for analyzing emerging devices: identifying the physical limitations of a
device, understanding how its architecture addresses those limitations, evaluating the associated
trade-oﬀs, and developing informed ideas for new device concepts.

Reading material :

The course will rely primarily on research papers, supplemented by several books and reference
texts.

1.  J.-P. Colinge, Ed., FinFETs and Other Multi-Gate Transistors, Springer, 2008.
2.  P. Avouris, T. F. Heinz, and T. Low, Eds., 2D Materials: Properties and Devices, Cambridge
University Press, 2017.
3. M. Houssa, A. Dimoulas, and A. Molle, Eds., 2D Materials for Nanoelectronics, CRC Press,
2016.
4.  D. Jena, Quantum Physics of Semiconductor Materials and Devices, Oxford University Press,
2022.
5.  J. H. Davies, The Physics of Low-Dimensional Semiconductors: An Introduction, Cambridge
University Press, 1998.
