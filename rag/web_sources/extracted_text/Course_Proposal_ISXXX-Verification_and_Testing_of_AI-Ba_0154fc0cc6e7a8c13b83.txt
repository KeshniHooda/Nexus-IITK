Course title: Verification and Testing of AI-Based Systems 
Course prerequisites: An introductory course on machine learning (or equivalent); programming

Other departments interested in the proposed course: CSE 

Course description: 
 
a. Objectives: Machine-learning components are increasingly being deployed in safety-critical and 
high-stakes systems, creating new challenges for verification and testing. Unlike conventional 
software, learned models are often not derived from explicit, human-written specifications, and 
their behavior depends on data, training procedures, and complex model architectures. 
Consequently, traditional verification and testing approaches must be adapted and extended to 
provide meaningful assurance of AI-based systems. 
This course aims to equip students with the principles and techniques needed to systematically 
verify and test AI-based systems and assess their readiness for deployment. Students will learn 
to formulate testable requirements and formal properties for machine-learning components; test 
datasets and deep neural networks using white-box and black-box techniques while efficiently 
prioritizing tests under limited labeling budgets; and evaluate properties such as robustness, 
uncertainty, and fairness. The course will also cover formal verification of neural networks, 
including quantized networks, as well as verification and testing of systems incorporating neural 
components. For closed-loop and autonomous systems, students will study reachability analysis, 
falsification, scenario-based testing, and runtime assurance to establish safety and reliability 
under diverse operating conditions. 
 
b. Logistics: The course will serve as a Department Elective Course for the IS PG students. 
Undergraduate students will be able to take this course as an Open Elective course. There is no 
scheduled laboratory slot; hands-on work is done through take-home programming assignments 
and a team project using open-source tools that run on freely available GPU resources.

c. Content: There will be an equivalent of 39 lectures of 50 minutes each over 13 weeks. The 
content is organised into six major topics, most of which span several weeks: foundations of V&V 
for AI-based systems; testing data for ML; testing deep neural networks; robustness, uncertainty 
and fairness; formal verification of neural networks; and verification and testing of systems with 
neural components. A weekly breakup of content is given below. 
 
d. Evaluation: The evaluation will be based on programming assignments, a course project 
(proposal, test plan, verification results, final report, presentation), a mid-semester examination 
and an end-semester examination.
