Course Description 
The course offers a socio-legal perspective on the transformation of the institutions of family, 
kinship and marriage. As these traditional social institutions transmute into their modern 
avatar, the nature of this transformation calls for sociological unpacking. Here, two distinct sets 
of normative frames operate in collocation—societal and legal. This leads to a contestation that 
Emile Durkheim had identified as anomie, a hallmark of all societies transitioning from status 
to contract. Taking a comparative lens, the course traces these changes in India since the 
colonial period. We undertake this exercise by analyzing the legal developments in the domain 
of family and kinship from the nineteenth century to the present. This broad overview will 
equip the students to appreciate the importance and impact of legislative changes on family 
and kinship. At the same time, the course will delve into a detailed analysis of specific reforms—
marriage, divorce and succession acts—to understand how the religion-based family law system 
works in India and its broader societal impacts, including the economy. The effect of family 
law reform on gender will be discussed in detail. This course will help students understand how 
family and kinship are affected by recent legal changes (the Uniform Civil Code, amendments 
to the Succession Act, the Senior Citizen Act, among others) and their future directions.
