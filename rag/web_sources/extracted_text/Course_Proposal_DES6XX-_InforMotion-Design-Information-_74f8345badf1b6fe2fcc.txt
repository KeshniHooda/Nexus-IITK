6. Course Description: 
A) Objectives 
1. 
To build a foundational understanding of data, information and visualisation, and of how 
quantitative and qualitative information complement each other. 
2. To develop the ability to interpret datasets and qualitative material (interviews, observations, 
images and documents), and to integrate evidence from these sources. 
3. To enable students to select and design visual representations and narratives, including time-
based and motion forms, suited to the audience and purpose. 
4. To encourage critical reflection on bias, uncertainty, accessibility and ethics in representing 
information.

C) Pre-requisites: 
Familiarity with digital design tools and design fundamentals, such as typography, colour theory, and 
layout, is desirable. 
D) Short Summary for Course Booklet 
This course explores how quantitative, qualitative and mixed information can be interpreted and 
communicated through visual design, storytelling and motion. Students learn to read data critically, 
analyse qualitative material, identify patterns and insights, and select appropriate forms of 
information visualisation. Through weekly studio practice, students apply principles of visual 
representation, narrative, and motion to communicate relationships, meaning and change. The course 
emphasises audience, context, accessibility and responsible representation, culminating in a final 
project that may take the form of a static, interactive or time-based information experience.

8. Any other remarks 
The studio hours combine project work with guided practice using graphics and motion-design tools, 
such as Illustrator, Cavalry, Blender and After Effects, introduced progressively through the semester. 
Assessment is based on assignments, studio practice and a final project that may draw on quantitative, 
qualitative or mixed information. Quantitative analysis is limited to foundational data literacy and 
descriptive methods; advanced statistics and programming are not prerequisites. Any interviews or 
observations involving participants must be conducted with appropriate consent, and personal 
information should be anonymised where appropriate.
