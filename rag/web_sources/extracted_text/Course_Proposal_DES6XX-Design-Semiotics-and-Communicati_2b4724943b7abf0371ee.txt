5. Course Description: 
 
Semiotics and Communication Theory examines how meaning is created, communicated and 
interpreted through signs, symbols, language and visual representations. The course 
introduces fundamental concepts of semiotics and semantics, followed by major 
communication models and their relevance to design. It explores how perception, culture, 
emotion and experience influence the interpretation of products, images and messages. 
Through lectures, visual analysis, case studies and studio exercises, students learn to apply 
theoretical concepts into design and develop a critical understanding of communication in 
everyday life and professional practices. 
 
6. Objectives 
 
The course aims to develop an understanding of semiotics, semantics and communication 
processes, with particular attention to their application in design. The objectives are to: 
 
1. Understand the fundamental concepts of semiotics, including signs, signification and the 
construction of meaning. 
2. Study communication models and examine their relevance to visual communication. 
3. Understand the influence of perception, culture, emotion and context on the 
interpretation of signs and messages. 
4. Examine how meaning is developed and communicated through products, images and 
interactive experiences. 
5. Apply semiotic methods and communication theories to analyse and develop designs.

Pre-requisites: 
 
None. Basic familiarity with design processes and the principles of visual design is desirable. 
 
Learning Outcomes: 
Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to: 
 
• Identify and interpret signs, symbols and relationships that contribute to the construction 
of meaning. 
• Explain major communication models and analyse their relevance to different 
communication situations. Further they can apply the same in design processes. 
• Examine how perception, culture, emotion and context influence the interpretation of 
meaning in products, images and messages. 
• Develop and present design solutions supported by an understanding of meaning, 
audience and communication context. 
 
Assessment Criteria and Weightage: 
• End Semester Exam: 10% 
• Assignments: 50% 
• Presentations: 20% 
• Project: 20% 
 
8. Short Summary for Course Booklet 
 
This course explores how signs, symbols and communication processes create meaning in 
design. Students will study semiotic concepts, communication models and the influence of 
perception, culture and emotion on interpretation. Through visual analysis, case studies and 
studio projects, they will learn to apply these theories to products, graphic design, branding 
and interactive experiences.

10. 
Any other remarks 
The course combines theoretical discussions with practical exercises, case studies and studio 
projects. Students will examine how signs and messages operate in different social and 
cultural settings and how design decisions influence interpretation. The practical component 
will encourage students to use semiotic methods and communication models in their design 
process, connecting theoretical understanding with the analysis and development of visual 
and interactive communication.
