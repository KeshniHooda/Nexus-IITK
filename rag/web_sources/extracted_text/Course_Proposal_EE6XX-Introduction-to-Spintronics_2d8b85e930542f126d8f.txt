6. Course Description: 
A) Objectives: 
Electron transport has long been central to microelectronics and VLSI. Each electron also carries spin 
angular momentum, which can be harnessed for data storage and processing. This course introduces 
the key principles of spin transport in heterostructures, providing students in Electrical Engineering 
and related disciplines with the foundation needed to pursue research in this area. Beginning with a 
review of essential quantum mechanics concepts, the course will gradually introduce the properties 
of quantum materials and devices, including the coupling among charge, spin, and orbital degrees of 
freedom and their interactions with magnons and phonons. 
The course is organized into four modules. The first introduces the essential concepts of quantum 
mechanics that provide the foundation for the course and will span 6–7 lectures. The second covers 
the fundamentals of nanomagnetism over 6–7 lectures. The third focuses on the core concepts of 
spintronics, including spin currents, spin torques, and spin-torque-induced magnetization dynamics. 
Topics include spin-torque ferromagnetic resonance, magnetization switching, and oscillations, 
covered over 8–9 lectures. The fourth module covers advanced topics, including spin–orbit coupling 
effects, spintronic devices, and their applications. 
In addition to standard homework exercises, students will undertake four mini-projects involving 
macrospin and quantum simulations. These projects will help them connect the theoretical concepts 
with physical behaviour and develop practical simulation skills. Students will also deliver a seminar 
and submit a report on assigned research topics. The course is designed for students with limited prior 
exposure to spintronics, guiding them from its basic principles to advanced concepts and practical 
applications.
