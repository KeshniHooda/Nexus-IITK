6. Course Description: 
A) Objectives: 
The course investigates a foundational work in philosophical aesthetics, Immanuel Kant’s 
Critique of Aesthetic Judgment (Part One of the Critique of Judgment). 
The course closely and critically considers Kant’s influential treatment of such 
important questions as the following: What is beauty? Is beauty a property of the object or 
does it lie in the beholder’s eye? Is taste innate or acquired? If I find something beautiful, can 
I expect all of you to agree with me? It is said there is ordinary perception and there is 
imaginative perception—what’s the difference? Are the pleasures given by a soothingly 
pretty flower and a scarily howling storm of the same kind? What is the ground of debates 
about beautiful nature or meaningful artworks? What makes nature’s beauty different from 
art’s beauty? What does it mean to say that a poem or a painting is profound? Why do some 
artworks invite multiple readings or become classics? Is aesthetic experience a form of 
consumption? Is there really an aesthetic way of encountering the world that is distinct from 
practical, intellectual, or moral engagement with it—don’t these approaches or spheres 
intersect? Can an aesthetic experience be a result of conscious effort or is it received like a 
gift? 
The course analyses and interrogates Kant’s concepts of aesthetic judgment, 
disinterested delight, subjective universality of aesthetic assessment, fine art, genius, aesthetic 
ideas, the beautiful and the sublime, purposiveness without purpose, form, reflection and

cognitive faculties. 
While philosophically re-enacting his views, the course engages in a contemporary 
conversation with Kant’s work.
