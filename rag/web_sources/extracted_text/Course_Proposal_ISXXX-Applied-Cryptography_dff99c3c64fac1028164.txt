6. Course Description: 
A) Objectives: The objective of this course is to introduce the students to the design and analysis of cryptographic 
algorithms. Students will learn techniques of symmetric and assymetric key cryptography, and understand how 
real-world protocols are built on top of these. 
 
 B) Contents: (for 13 weeks of lectures) 
 
Numbers in square brackets [] against each topic indicate the number of lectures/labs for that topic. The 
focus of the course is on specific designs and their analysis. 
(The course differs from the other course CS641 already available in the curriculum significantly as the treatment of 
CS641 is with emphasis on proofs of security of various primitives and constructions whereas this course focuses on 
specific designs, attacks, and implementation issues.) 
(1) Introduction [6]: Historical ciphers and their cryptanalysis, Perfect Security and stream ciphers. 
(2) Symmetric Key Cryptography [9]: Block ciphers, hash functions, MAC, AE schemes 
(3) Asymmetric Key Cryptography [12]: Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange, RSA, El-Gamal cryptosystem, ECC, Digital 
Signatures, Certificates and PKI. 
(4) Cryptanalysis [12]: Techniques like linear and differential attacks, MITM, vulnerabilities in real-life 
protocols/tools such as WEP, Bluetooth, WinZip etc. 
 
C) Pre-requisites: 
• 
None 
D) Short summary for including in the Courses of Study Booklet: This course introduces students to the design and 
analysis of cryptographic algorithms with more emphasis on use and applications of various constructions. The 
course focusses on how real-life protocols and tools may become vulnerable due to weaknesses in these 
algorithms.
