10. Course Description: 
 
Mobility has been an integral part of the human evolution and has always played 
a critical role in socio-economic growth of a society or a country. With increased 
global attention on environment and sustainability mobility’s contribution to air 
pollution and environmental degradation is studied, but it is not studied/researched 
independently keeping it at the centerstage. A course on Transformative Mobility is 
timely because world is in the middle of a new mobility revolution where mobility is 
one of the primary drivers for economic growth, ease of living and reducing 
emissions. Mobility, which has always played an important role in evolution and 
growth of a society, is no longer just about transportation. With the emergence and 
proliferation of cutting-edge technologies coupled with their tremendous impact on 
economy and citizens’ life, mobility is reshaped by clean energy, urbanization, climate 
goals, geo-political considerations, changing business models and new policy

[Page 2]
frameworks. It is important that we study all the facets of changes taking place in 
the movement of people and goods and how is it impacting the socio-economic 
fabric of society and country at large. This course will provide an insight into the 
paradigm shift in mobility, rapid technological disruptions, sustainability and 
climate change related issues, changing travel patterns because of urbanization, 
digital and economic transformation of transportation and above all the policy and 
governance challenges. It is expected to provide an integrated perspective as it 
integrates Engineering, Economics, Environmental Science, Urban Planning, Data 
Science, Behavioral Science, Public Policy and Business management. 
Affordable, accessible, inclusive and safe mobility solutions are important strategic 
levers for rapid economic development and improving ‘Ease of Living’ in a country. 
Shared, connected and clean mobility is increasingly becoming the key principles of 
effective mobility solutions across the world. Given our country’s current economic 
growth, its commitment to climate goals, it would be pertinent to formulate, study 
and analyse our strategies to place ourself as a key driver of the sustainable mobility 
revolution in the world. 
11. Expected Learning Outcomes: 
At the end of this course the student is expected to be able to understand mobility 
as a system that enhances access, opportunity, productivity and quality of life. What 
emerging technologies, public policies, climate considerations, urbanization, 
changing geo-political equations and other forces are transforming the way people 
and goods move in a country. The changing role of mobility in the development of a 
country and the complexities in framing policies related with mobility and 
transportation for a country along with related sustainability issues. The student is 
expected to be able to evaluate technologies, understand urbanization and public 
policy issues impacting mobility paradigm.

13. Remarks: 
 
India is now the third largest automobile manufacturer in the world, is the fastest 
growing automobile market and is the fastest growing aviation market in terms of 
passenger traffic, with largest population in the world and being the fastest growing 
large economy, its performance on indicators of peoples’ and goods movement is 
low. Movement of people and goods has a direct correlation with economic growth, 
with that perspective, country is making heavy capital expenditure (>$100B+ 
annually) and doing aggressive policy restructuring, to increase efficiencies 
dramatically (cut down transport delays etc.), though it has to bridge a vast gap to 
meet global efficiency benchmarks. Mobility as a sector is bound to grow and gain 
importance. The course is to expose students to a newer area which is an integral 
part of economic activity and more importantly a major contributor to air 
pollution. Disruptive and major changes are taking place in the movement of 
people and freight and how to make it sustainable without making it economically 
unviable is a major challenge to the world and particularly to our country, having 
the largest pool of people, who are gradually moving upwards in financial ladder. 
The course will also have participation of practitioners, policy makers, start-ups and 
industry experts in proposed workshops and seminars.
