6. Course Description: 
Agricultural food systems have evolved over centuries to ensure food and nutritional 
security. An agricultural farm is the basic unit of this food production system that 
involves natural, economic, social, and technological factors. The complex interactions 
among these factors must be understood to ensure the successful design of agricultural 
farms and achieve self-sufficiency in food production. The course aims to provide a 
comprehensive understanding of the evolution of farm design throughout the ages, in 
light of the technological knowledge and resources available at the time. This course is 
of immense significance for designing food security systems worldwide and for 
promoting agricultural entrepreneurship in the evolving global environment. 
 
 A) Objectives: 
a. To understand the fundamentals of farm design and cropping patterns 
b. To evaluate the role of farm design in the agro-industrial ecosystem

[Page 2]
c. To analyse the complex interplay of factors governing the dynamic machinery of a 
farm 
d. To design farms with the intent of creating regenerative food production entities

C) Pre-requisites: None 
D) Short summary for inclusion in the Courses of Study Booklet: 
The design of farms and cropping patterns is central to food production, where natural, 
economic, social, and technological factors work together to ensure food and nutritional 
security. This course provides a comprehensive understanding of the fundamentals of farm 
design and cropping patterns from a systems perspective in an agro-industrial ecosystem. 
It aims to train students to design farms as regenerative entities
