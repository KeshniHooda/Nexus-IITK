5. Course Description: 
 
Type Design and Font Engineering introduces students from the design of letterforms to the 
development of digital fonts. The course combines an understanding of the history and 
principles of type design with practical exercises in drawing, constructing and refining 
glyphs. Students will learn how to develop a consistent set of characters and convert their 
designs into functional digital fonts. The course also introduces font encoding, character 
mapping, spacing, kerning and OpenType features. 
 
6. Objectives 
 
The course aims to develop an understanding of typeface design and the technical processes 
involved in producing digital fonts. The objectives are to: 
• Understand the history of type design and the influence of writing systems, printing 
technologies and digital tools on its development. 
• Study the structure of letterforms and the principles that contribute to a consistent 
typeface. 
• Develop skills in drawing, constructing and refining glyphs for a selected script. 
• Understand the technical aspects of font production, including character encoding, glyph 
mapping, spacing, kerning and OpenType features. 
• Develop the ability to produce, test and refine functional digital fonts for print and digital 
applications.

Pre-requisites: 
 
None (Basic familiarity with design processes is desirable) 
 
Learning Outcomes: 
Upon completion of the course, students will be able to: 
• Explain the historical development of type production and the principles of type design. 
• Understand and describe how digital outlines are constructed, scaled and rendered on 
different output devices. 
• Construct and refine glyphs while maintaining consistency across a character set. 
• Understand and develop skill of spacing, kerning and hinting of digital letters. 
• Evaluate the quality and legibility of typefaces across different sizes and applications.

8. Short Summary for Course Booklet 
 
This course introduces students to the fundamentals, history and practice of typography, 
with an emphasis on print and digital media. It develops skills in letterform design, type 
composition and layout with Latin and non-Latin typefaces. The course adopts a practice-
oriented approach to help students understand the role of typography in communication.

10. 
Any other remarks 
The course combines theoretical study with studio practice to help students understand the 
relationship between typeface design and digital font technology. Students will examine how 
the construction, spacing and rendering of letterforms influence typographic quality and 
legibility. The final project will involve developing a typeface, producing a functional digital 
font and evaluating its performance across different sizes and applications.
