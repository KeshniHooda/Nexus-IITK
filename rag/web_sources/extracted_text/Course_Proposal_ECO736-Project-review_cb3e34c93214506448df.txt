10. Course Description: The course is designed to allow students to do an intensive literature 
review on their area of interest, and initiate discussions with their prospective supervisors and 
present their work. 
 
A) Objectives: The main objective of this course is to let PhD students start the literature review 
for their thesis at an early stage (before completing their comprehensive examination). 
Additionally, this course helps the student get feedback on their tentative research questions and 
pinpoints their question of interest at an early stage. This course will help a PhD student set up a 
regular schedule for their work, build rapport with their prospective supervisor and maintain a 
routine of meetings with their prospective supervisor. By the end of this course, a PhD student will 
have learnt how to write a paper that is acceptable for publication in a peer-reviewed journal, by 
going through multiple peer-reviewed papers and preparing their own literature review paper. The 
student will also present their literature review to a committee who will provide feedback on both 
their presentation skills and possible research questions for their thesis. 
 
B) Contents: 
Each PhD student will be evaluated by their prospective supervisor (henceforth referred to as 
supervisor) based on the frequency of meetings, the reports submitted and a presentation. 
 
Evaluation has 3 main components – meetings, reports and presentations:

[Page 2]
• Each student must have at least one meeting per week with their supervisor (excluding 
semester breaks). Duration of the meeting will be decided by the supervisor (but should 
be at least 50 minutes). The topic of discussion of each meeting will also be decided by 
the supervisor, and the broad areas of discussion may include (but is not limited to) the 
student’s research area of interest, papers recommended for the literature review, 
discussion about said papers, any guidance material on presenting and writing papers, etc. 
The supervisor will evaluate the student based on their interactions, their progress and 
regularity of these meetings. 
 
• The student will also be evaluated on a set of reports submitted throughout the semester. 
All reports must be submitted to the supervisor to evaluate. The reports must also be cc-
ed (or if hard copy, submitted with supervisor’s signature) to the instructor in charge to 
record that the student has met the deadlines for the reports: 

1. The first report must include the title of the project, the supervisor’s name and a brief (1 
page or 500 words) outline of the research area of interest. If the student has already started 
working on the project in the previous semester, they must include what has been done up 
till now and what their tentative plan is. The report must be submitted on or before the last 
date of dropping a course for that semester. If the report is not received within the deadline, 
the student will be deregistered from the course. 
2. A mid-semester report must be submitted by the student, detailing their progress up till 
then (1 page or 500 words). The deadline for submission will be the last day of mid-
semester exams for that semester. 
3. An end-semester report must be submitted that includes the literature review by the 
student. The report must be a maximum of 8 pages (including graphs and figures). The 
deadline for submission will be the last date of end-semester exams for that semester. 
 
• A short presentation of the literature review (12 minutes presentation+3 minutes Q&A) by 
the student at the end of the course will be evaluated by a 2-member review committee. 
The 2-member review committee will include (1) the prospective supervisor, and (2) either 
the DPGC or another faculty member (if there are overlaps in positions). The presentation 
will be jointly evaluated by the student’s review committee. The presentation should 
ideally take place within the week of submission of the final report. 
 
C) Prerequisites: NA 
 
D) Short summary for including in the Courses of Study Booklet: This course allows a PhD 
student to start working on their thesis at an early stage (before completing their comprehensives) 
by reviewing the literature of their research area/question of interest. The students additionally get 
feedback on their research ideas during regular meetings with their prospective supervisor and 
learns how to write and present a literature review paper. This course also helps students set up a 
routine for their PhD, by learning how to manage time for meetings and work.
